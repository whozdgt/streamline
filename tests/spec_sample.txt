1 History
1.1 Africa and empire
1.1.1 The Berlin Conference
Students should understand why the European powers met in Berlin in 1884 to 1885, the principle of effective occupation and the partition of Africa.
1.1.2 The Congo Free State
The rule of King Leopold II over the Congo, rubber quotas, forced labour and the international response.
1.2 The Cold War
1.2.1 Origins of the Cold War
The breakdown of the wartime alliance after 1945, Yalta and Potsdam, the Truman Doctrine, Marshall Aid and the policy of containment.
1.2.2 Crises of the Cold War
The Berlin Blockade and Airlift of 1948 to 1949 and the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962, and how each was resolved.
2 Biology
2.1 Cells
2.1.1 Cell structure
Eukaryotic animal and plant cells: nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ribosomes, cell wall, chloroplasts and vacuole.
2.1.2 Cell division
Chromosomes, the cell cycle and mitosis, producing two genetically identical diploid cells.
2.1.3 Transport in cells
Diffusion, osmosis across a partially permeable membrane, and active transport using energy from respiration.
3 Chemistry
3.1 Bonding
3.1.1 Ionic bonding
Transfer of electrons between metals and non-metals to form ions held in a giant ionic lattice by electrostatic forces.
3.1.2 Covalent bonding
Shared pairs of electrons between non-metal atoms in simple molecules and giant covalent structures such as diamond.
3.1.3 Metallic bonding
Positive metal ions in a sea of delocalised electrons; conductivity and alloys.
4 English Literature
4.1 Shakespeare
4.1.1 Macbeth
Themes of ambition, guilt and the supernatural; the characters of Macbeth, Lady Macbeth and the witches; Shakespeare's language.
5 Physics
5.1 Forces
5.1.1 Newton's laws of motion
Resultant force, acceleration and the relationship F = ma; inertia and action and reaction forces.
