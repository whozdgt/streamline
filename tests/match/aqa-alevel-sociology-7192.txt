# A-level Sociology notes written for this test: essay plans, study summaries and lesson notes. Each is under the
# point it is about, numbered as the spec's A-level content is ("Theory and Methods" is in Paper 1 and Paper 3 alike).
=== 4.1.1 | education - class
Differential educational achievement by social class: material deprivation (Douglas, Howard), cultural deprivation (Bernstein's restricted and elaborated codes), cultural capital (Bourdieu), labelling and self-fulfilling prophecy (Becker, Rist), pupil subcultures (Lacey, Willis). Marketisation and the 1988 Education Reform Act, league tables, parentocracy.
=== 4.1.1 | functionalism and education
Functionalist view of education: Durkheim (social solidarity, specialist skills), Parsons (meritocracy, bridge between family and society), Davis and Moore (role allocation). Marxists Bowles and Gintis (correspondence principle, myth of meritocracy) and Willis (the lads). New Right: Chubb and Moe.
=== 4.1.2 | methods in context
Methods in context: using participant observation to study pupils in schools, issues of access, ethics with young people, the Hawthorne effect in classrooms; official statistics on exam results; questionnaires for teachers. Positivism vs interpretivism.
=== 4.2.2 | families
Couples and domestic division of labour: Young and Willmott's symmetrical family, Oakley's critique, dual burden, triple shift (Duncombe and Marsden). Changing patterns of marriage, divorce and cohabitation; family diversity (Rapoport). Childhood as a social construct (Aries, Postman). Demography: birth rates, ageing population.
=== 4.2.1 | culture and identity
Concepts of culture: high, popular, folk, global, subculture. Socialisation and the self. Identity and class, gender, ethnicity, sexuality, age, disability. Postmodern identities, consumption and leisure.
=== 4.2.5 | beliefs
Religion as a conservative force or a force for social change: functionalism (Durkheim's sacred and profane, Malinowski), Marxism (opium of the people), Weber's Protestant ethic, liberation theology. Secularisation debate (Bruce, Wilson), religious fundamentalism, new religious movements (Wallis), religiosity by gender and ethnicity.
=== 4.2.6 | global dev
Global development: modernisation theory (Rostow's five stages), dependency theory (Frank), world systems theory (Wallerstein), aid and debt, TNCs, NGOs, industrialisation, urbanisation, gender and development, globalisation.
=== 4.2.7 | media
Ownership and control of the media, pluralism vs Marxist manipulative and hegemonic models. News values (Galtung and Ruge), moral panics (Cohen's Folk Devils), representations of gender and ethnicity, media effects: hypodermic syringe, two-step flow, uses and gratifications, cultural effects.
=== 4.3.1 | crime
Crime and deviance: Merton's strain theory, Cohen's status frustration, Miller's focal concerns, labelling (Becker, Lemert's secondary deviance), Marxist and neo-Marxist (Taylor, Walton and Young), Left realism (Lea and Young relative deprivation) and Right realism. Ethnicity and crime, gender and crime, globalisation and green crime, state crime, surveillance (Foucault's panopticon).
=== 4.3.2,4.1.3 | theory and methods
Sociological theories: consensus vs conflict, structural vs action. Is sociology a science? Popper's falsification, Kuhn's paradigms. Objectivity and values (Weber, Gouldner). Sociology and social policy. Feminist theories: liberal, radical, Marxist, intersectional.
