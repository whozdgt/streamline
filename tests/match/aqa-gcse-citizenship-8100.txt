# GCSE Citizenship Studies notes written for this test: revision notes and practice answers. Each is under the point it
# is about.
=== 3.2.1 | british values
Principles and values that underpin British society: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, mutual respect and tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs.
=== 3.2.2 | identity
Identity: we all have multiple identities (national, regional, religious, ethnic). Migration to the UK, a diverse multicultural society, what it means to be British, and the changing make-up of the population.
=== 3.2.3 | media and the press
The free press and the media: the Leveson Inquiry into phone hacking, press regulation by IPSO, censorship, the role of the BBC, social media and fake news, the right to privacy versus freedom of expression.
=== 3.2.4 | international organisations
The UK's role in international organisations: the United Nations and the Security Council, NATO, the Commonwealth, the Council of Europe, the World Trade Organization, leaving the EU.
=== 3.3.1 | why we need laws
Why a society needs laws: to keep order, protect rights and settle disputes. Criminal law (the state prosecutes) and civil law (disputes between individuals), the sources of law.
=== 3.3.2 | rights in the legal system
Rights and responsibilities within the legal system: rights when arrested, the right to a fair trial, jury service, magistrates and Crown Court, the age of criminal responsibility is 10 in England and Wales.
=== 3.3.4 | human rights
Universal human rights: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights 1948, the European Convention on Human Rights, the Human Rights Act 1998, and how rights are protected by the courts.
=== 3.4.1 | power in the UK
Where political power resides: Parliament (Commons and Lords), the Prime Minister and the Cabinet, the monarch's role, the separation of powers, how Parliament holds the government to account.
=== 3.4.2 | local and devolved government
Local and devolved government: the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd in Wales, the Northern Ireland Assembly, local councils and council tax, elected mayors, how citizens can take part locally.
=== 3.4.4 | other systems
How others govern themselves: the USA's presidential system and its constitution compared with the UK's parliamentary system; dictatorships and one-party states.
=== 3.4.5 | political change
How citizens bring about political change: voting, standing for election, joining pressure groups like Greenpeace, e-petitions, protest marches, lobbying MPs.
=== 3.5.2 | our campaign
Taking citizenship action: our group's campaign to ban single-use plastic in the school canteen — research, a survey of students, a letter to the head and the local MP, and evaluating the impact of our action.
