=== 3.1.1.1 | metals and alloys
Ferrous metals contain iron: mild steel, cast iron, stainless steel. Non-ferrous: aluminium, copper, brass (copper and zinc). An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements to improve properties, e.g. steel is harder and stronger than iron; aluminium alloys are light for aircraft frames.
=== 3.1.1.2 | polymers
Thermoplastics (ABS, HIPS, acrylic, nylon) soften when heated and can be reshaped; thermosetting polymers (epoxy resin, urea formaldehyde, melamine) set permanently and don't melt again, so they're used for electrical fittings and kitchen worktops.
=== 3.1.1.3 | composites
Composite materials combine two materials: carbon fibre reinforced polymer (CFRP) is stiff and very light for racing bike frames; glass reinforced plastic (GRP) for boat hulls; reinforced concrete has steel bars to take tension.
=== 3.2.1 | additive manufacturing
3D printing builds a part layer by layer from a CAD file: FDM melts plastic filament, SLA cures resin with a laser, SLS fuses powder. Good for rapid prototyping and complex shapes, slow for mass production.
=== 3.2.5 | joining
Joining methods: permanent (MIG and TIG welding, brazing, soldering, riveting, adhesives like epoxy) and temporary (nuts and bolts, machine screws). Welding melts the parent metals; soldering uses a filler with a lower melting point.
=== 3.3.1 | mechanical systems
Levers (first, second and third class), mechanical advantage = load / effort. Gear trains: gear ratio = teeth on driven gear / teeth on driver; idler gears change direction. Cams turn rotary motion into reciprocating motion; pulleys and belts.
=== 3.3.2 | electrical systems
Ohm's law V = I x R; power P = I x V. Series circuits share voltage, parallel circuits share current. DC motors, solenoids and relays; calculating the current a 12 V, 24 W bulb draws.
=== 3.3.3 | electronic systems
Electronic systems: input, process, output. Sensors: LDR for light, thermistor for temperature. Logic gates AND, OR, NOT with truth tables; programmable microcontrollers (PIC) controlling an LED and buzzer.
=== 3.3.5 | pneumatics
Pneumatic systems use compressed air: compressor, single-acting and double-acting cylinders, 3/2 and 5/2 valves, push-button and lever-operated valves. Used in bus doors and factory automation.
=== 3.4.2 | testing
Destructive tests: tensile test pulls a sample until it breaks to find tensile strength, hardness tests (Brinell, Rockwell). Non-destructive testing: ultrasound and X-ray to find internal cracks in welds.
=== 3.4.3 | aerodynamics
Aerodynamics: drag slows a vehicle, lift on an aircraft wing; streamlining a car body reduces drag and fuel use; wind tunnel testing with smoke, and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations.
=== 3.5 | modern technologies
Impact of modern technologies: automation and robots on car production lines, CAD/CAM, the effect on jobs and skills, sustainability, recycling and the life cycle of products.
